# M17 on the Ulefone Armor 26 Ultra radio — deep dive (2026-10-22)

M17 is an open digital voice mode: 4FSK at 4800 symbols/s (±2.4 kHz for the outer symbols), root-raised-cosine shaped
(α 0.5), 40 ms frames with a Golay-coded link channel and a K=5 convolutional code, Codec2 3200 voice. Real M17 radios
modulate and demodulate it directly (a flat FM modulator and discriminator, DC to 3.6 kHz). This radio module only
offers its voice audio path: 8 kHz audio in and out through the phone. This study measured both directions on the
owner's Armor (VFO 467.625 MHz, wide), with a HackRF sending real M17 transmissions to the phone (receive) and
capturing the phone's transmissions (transmit). Tools and our recordings: `tools/m17-research/` (README there).

## Receive: works, at the standard deviation

Receive-path response measured with a 20 Hz – 3.8 kHz sweep, relative to 1 kHz: **below 250 Hz −25 to −37 dB** (a steep
high-pass, most likely the sub-audible/CTCSS filter), 300 Hz – 3 kHz within about 6 dB, noise above about 3.2 kHz.
M17 carries a lot of energy below 300 Hz, so the plain M17 demodulator (matched filter + slicer) decoded **nothing**
(0 of 100 frames at every level).

The same approach that rescued POCSAG works here: a decision-feedback equalizer (DFE) — a feed-forward part over ±4
symbols at two samples per symbol, plus 16–48 past symbol decisions that rebuild the missing low frequencies — with
cubic and |x|·x terms for the receiver's compression at full deviation, trained once on one known transmission and then
run on other, independent transmissions with its own decisions and per-frame timing tracking (the phone's audio clock
drifts about 15 ppm against the transmitter):

| Test transmission (independent payloads) | Symbol errors | M17 decode (LSF CRC, exact stream frames) |
|---|---|---|
| ±1.2 kHz, ±0.96 kHz (40–60 %) | 0.01 % | LSF ok, **100 of 100** |
| ±1.9 kHz (80 %) | 0.01–0.24 % | LSF ok, **100 of 100** |
| ±2.4 kHz (standard), two more transmissions | 0.60–0.71 % | LSF ok, **100 of 100** each |

Limits: the equalizer is calibrated per phone (one clean full-deviation transmission from a HackRF), and how well a
calibration transfers depends on which transmission it was trained on (training on a different full-deviation recording
gave 86–98 of 100); combining several calibration recordings needs a common timing reference (not finished). For an app,
an **adaptive** equalizer (decision-directed, kept up to date with the frames the error correction confirms) is the
robust design. The signal was about −94 dBm at the phone (HackRF indoors, phone outside); weaker signals were not tested.

## Transmit: not feasible for standard M17 receivers (through the audio path)

> **Update 2026-10-24:** this verdict holds for the audio path only. A firmware route around it is proven: a host-keyed
> patch drives the HR-C7000 DMR modulator from TX RAM, and M17 link-setup and stream frames decoded on the air at
> Golay error 0 (R4d–R4k). See `radio_firmware/HANDOFF.md` (route T1), `radio_firmware/results.md` and
> `radio_firmware/reviews.md`. It is not a feature yet: streaming symbols over the UART, a spec-written M17 encoder in
> SignalDeck, the 30 s time-out and the opt-in loader are still open.

Transmit-path response (audio PCM in → FM deviation, HackRF discriminator): **below 250 Hz essentially nothing
(about −60 dB)** — a hard sub-audible high-pass —, 400 Hz – 1 kHz flat at about 0.45–0.48 Hz of deviation per PCM unit,
pre-emphasis above 1.5 kHz (+5 dB at 2 kHz, +10 dB at 2.4 kHz), and a **deviation limiter at about ±4.5 kHz** that the
pre-emphasized symbol transitions hit even at a low audio level. The radio also inverts the polarity. M17 sent as audio:

- plain M17 decode of the phone's transmission: **0 of 90 frames** at all three audio levels;
- even an equalizer trained on the exact transmission cannot fit it (mean-square error 0.65 against 0.02 on the receive
  side): the limiter's clipping destroys information;
- with the audio pre-shaped (pre-emphasis removed, cut above 2.8 kHz, aiming at ±1.2–2.4 kHz) the deviation still
  peaked at 4.1–4.4 kHz and nothing decoded: the transmit audio path compresses or limits before the modulator, beyond
  the linear filters measured.

The missing band below 250 Hz alone rules out standard M17 receivers (they do not equalize; it would need about 1000×
the drive). Between two phones that both run an equalizer it might be possible if the limiter can be kept out of the
way (lower levels, gentler shaping): not tried further tonight.

## What an M17 receiver in SignalDeck would need

- the adaptive DFE receiver (from this study), per-phone calibration with a HackRF (or blind start-up from the preamble
  and sync words, not tried), timing tracking;
- the M17 frame decoder, written from the M17 specification (libm17 is GPL-2.0-only, which cannot go into the GPL-3
  app; the research script here follows the specification's tables);
- Codec2 3200 voice decoding (LGPL C library: needs the Android NDK, not installed on this machine, or a Java port);
- receive only: talk-back by M17 is not possible from this radio.
